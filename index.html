About

Research identity

I am a postdoctoral researcher at KAIST with a Ph.D. in Systems and Control Engineering. My research focuses on electrified propulsion systems, particularly the systematic design, modeling, optimization, and control of hybrid and extended-range electric vehicle powertrains.

My work spans the complete powertrain development chain: architecture exploration, component sizing, vehicle-level performance optimization, energy management, multi-mode transmission control, forward simulation, and hardware-in-the-loop validation. I am especially interested in methods that connect theoretical design-space exploration with implementable control strategies and real-world system validation.

Research

Research directions

My research connects powertrain architecture design, vehicle-level optimization, transient control, and experimental validation.

01

Electrified Powertrain Architecture Design

Systematic exploration and optimization of HEV, PHEV, and EREV architectures, including power-split, parallel, and multi-mode configurations.

02

Energy & Performance Optimization

Dynamic programming, ECMS, optimal control, component sizing, fuel-economy analysis, acceleration optimization, and vehicle-level performance assessment.

03

Transmission & Mode-Shift Control

Modeling and control of multi-mode transmissions, dog-clutch engagement, electronic synchronization, and transient powertrain behavior.

04

Modeling, Simulation & Validation

Forward and quasi-static vehicle simulation, real-time control implementation, HILS, dSPACE-based validation, and model-based controller development.

Projects

Selected research projects

Next-Generation EREV Architecture Exploration

Developing a unified design-space exploration and auto-sizing framework for extended-range electric vehicle architectures across series, parallel, and power-split operating modes.

High-Performance Multi-Mode Hybrid Powertrains

Integrating power-split and parallel operating modes to improve fuel economy and acceleration, while developing control strategies for dog-clutch-based mode transitions.

Dedicated Hybrid Transmission Design & Control

Modeled representative DHT systems, developed fuel-economy and acceleration assessment tools, derived implementable control strategies, and built a forward-looking simulator for clutch transients.

Virtual Design Space for Power-Split Hybrids

Developed systematic methods for exhaustive architecture exploration, topology selection, and automated physical realization of single- and multi-mode power-split hybrid systems.

Mode-Shift Control with Dog Clutches

Developed modeling and control strategies for engagement and disengagement of dog clutches in multi-mode hybrid transmissions, including synchronization and transient performance analysis.

Hardware-in-the-Loop Validation

Contributed to the development and use of reconfigurable HILS platforms for validating selected hybrid powertrain architectures and their supervisory control strategies.
